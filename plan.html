01Part I — The oldest program

Job — make "Sanskrit is programmable" feel earned, not a metaphor. This is the emotional core; give it the most room.

Draft opening: "Around 400 BCE, Pāṇini compressed the entire grammar of Sanskrit into ~4,000 rewrite rules — ordered, exception-handled, with metarules about how rules apply. It is the oldest formal system we have. For 2,300 years it could only run on people. This project ports a fragment of it to a machine."

  • One tight paragraph on what makes Pāṇini's grammar code: derivation, not description — forms are computed from stems + rules (show rāma + ins.sg → rāmeṇa, the ṇ appearing because a rule fires, not because a table says so).
  • What "formalized in Lean" means for a lay reader: a proof assistant is a program that checks proofs; we wrote the grammar as executable definitions, so "this sentence is grammatical" becomes a statement the machine can prove or refute. Gold paradigms are theorems, re-proved at every build.
  • The fragment, honestly: 7 noun classes × 8 cases × 3 numbers, present tense, ~100 words, sandhi undone at boundaries. A fragment — but a real one, and the method scales by adding rules, not data.

04What this shows — and doesn't

Job — credibility. Two claims, their limits, prior art.
  • Claim (i): a natural language's grammar — Pāṇini's Sanskrit, fragment — formalized in a general-purpose prover such that grammaticality is a kernel-checkable proposition. Prior art paragraph: GF / Coq-NLI did type-theoretic semantics; "to our knowledge, first machine-checked Pāṇinian fragment."
  • Claim (ii): the standard RLVR pipeline runs end-to-end against it — first prover-checked natural-language grammaticality reward (Lean rewards for math are common; the domain is the novelty).
  • Not claimed: all of Sanskrit; semantic understanding; that RL beats SFT here. The fragment's edges stated plainly.
  • Close on the sentiment, one line: "Pāṇini's grammar was always a program. It just waited 2,300 years for a runtime." ← candidate closing line / X-post kicker.

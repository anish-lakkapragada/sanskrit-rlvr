## Main post

Sanskrit is a programmable language. We ran it on a compiler.

Around 400 BCE, Pāṇini compressed Sanskrit into ~4,000 ordered rewrite rules —
the oldest formal system we have. It ran on human grammarians for 2,300 years.

We ported a fragment to Lean 4. This is now a theorem, checked by the kernel:

    example : Grammatical "rāmo grāmaṃ gacchati" := by native_decide

Swap one vowel (plural subject, singular verb) and the file does not build.

Then the RLVR loop, same recipe as the math models: an LLM writes Sanskrit,
the compiler grades it, zero human labels. On one MacBook, the share of its
sentences that compile went from 2% → 85%.

Grammar was always code. Now it has a runtime. ⟨link⟩

## Thread

1/ The part that matters: nothing in the verifier is a lookup table. rāmeṇa's
retroflex ṇ is *derived* — a rule fires because of the r earlier in the word,
exactly as Pāṇini wrote it. Declension, conjugation, sandhi-undoing, agreement:
~600 lines of Lean, no Mathlib, and the paradigm tables are theorems re-proved
at every build.

2/ The loop: model proposes 6 answers → Lean judges each in 20 ms (grammatical?
right words in the right cases?) → reinforce the above-average. Tasks and gold
answers are generated by the formalization itself — the grammar is its own
teacher.

3/ A curiosity we tested: supervised examples vs RL, same compiler signal.
Distillation does the lifting (2% → 85% in two epochs on grammar-generated
answers). RL alone can't bootstrap (2% → 3%; nothing correct to reinforce).
RL after distillation polishes at the margin. If you can enumerate the truth,
teach by example — RL starts where enumeration ends.

4/ Favorite snapshot: before, the model writes "kanyāḥ candraṃ dṛśanti" —
an invented verb form, fails to build. After: "kanyāḥ candram paśyanti" — it
learned that dṛś 'to see' takes the suppletive stem paśya-, and Lean accepts
the proof.

5/ Scope, honestly: a fragment — 7 noun classes, present tense, ~100 words.
Prior art: type-theoretic *semantics* (GF, Coq-NLI) exists; to our knowledge
this is the first machine-checked Pāṇinian fragment in a prover, and the first
RLVR run rewarding prover-checked natural-language grammaticality.

6/ Everything open: the Lean library, one Python file of glue, the evals, and
a visual report. ⟨repo link⟩

Pāṇini's grammar was always a program. It just waited 2,300 years for a
runtime.

Nothing to Spare:
Every Marker of the Śivasūtras is Load-Bearing

The Sanskrit tradition says Pāṇini wastes nothing. For his sound-alphabet, that is now a machine-checked theorem — try to delete something and watch the grammar break.

1.The claim

Pāṇini's grammar of Sanskrit (4th c. BCE) opens with the Śivasūtras: all the sounds of the language in 14 short lines, each closed by a dummy marker letter (an anubandha, shown dark below). Any contiguous stretch — a start sound plus a marker — names a class of sounds the grammar needs: aC = the vowels, haL = the consonants, and 41 more. The whole apparatus costs 14 markers plus one duplicated sound (the h, listed twice).

“Grammarians rejoice over the saving of half a mora as over the birth of a son.” — the lāghava (economy) maxim of the Pāṇinian tradition

Economy cuts both ways: it's not just that no cheaper scheme exists — it's that Pāṇini's own scheme carries no dead weight. Every marker, and the one duplication, must be doing real work. That's the theorem on this page: delete any one of the 15 pieces of apparatus and some class the grammar actually uses becomes unnameable.

3.What exactly was proved

A pratyāhāra is well-formed when its marker actually occurs after its start sound (the traditional reading — the reason the final sūtra h L exists at all). The theorem, for each marker m of the 14:

-- delete any single anubandha: some attested class loses its encoding
theorem no_marker_redundant :
    ∀ m : Marker, ¬ IsSAlphabetT (removeMarker shivasutras m) paniniClasses

-- and the duplicated h can't go either — under ANY reading of pratyāhāras
theorem noH2_not_lax : ¬ IsSAlphabet shivasutras_noH2 paniniClasses

Each case is decided by exhaustive kernel computation: the truncated alphabet is a concrete list, "some class breaks" is witnessed by a concrete class, and "no encoding rescues it" is a search over all 42 × 14 = 588 sound–marker pairs, replayed inside Lean's proof kernel. Why this is more than a party trick. Together with the optimality theorems on the companion branch (no rival scheme uses fewer duplications; every rival needs ≥ 11 markers), this closes the economy story from both sides: Pāṇini's Śivasūtras are as cheap as possible, and everything they do spend is spent on something. The second half — irredundancy — appears to be stated as a per-marker theorem here for the first time.

4.Bonus: the kernel does the philology's arithmetic

How many pratyāhāras do the Śivasūtras support in principle? The literature (Petersen 2004, fn. 2) says 305 pairs, 14 of them naming single sounds. Those counts tally sound occurrences; if a pratyāhāra always starts at a sound's first occurrence (as our model — and the tradition's reading of haL — has it), the pair formed at the second h merges with the first. The kernel confirms the shifted counts exactly:

theorem card_wellFormed_pratyaharas : wellFormedPairs.card = 304
theorem card_singleton_pratyaharas : … = 13

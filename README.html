ह य व र — किमर्थम्?

Forced or Free?
How Determined is the Order of Pāṇini's Sound-Alphabet

Scholars have argued for decades — and Patañjali for two millennia — about why the Śivasūtras run in exactly this order. For every neighbouring pair of sounds, the Lean kernel now says whether swapping them is fatal or harmless.

1.The question

Pāṇini's grammar of Sanskrit (4th c. BCE) opens with the Śivasūtras: all the sounds of the language in 14 short lines, each closed by a dummy marker letter (dark below). Any stretch from a sound to a later marker names a class the grammar uses — aC = the vowels, haL = the consonants, and 41 more. For the whole scheme to work, every needed class must sit contiguously somewhere in the list. That constrains the order. But by how much?

This is a real scholarly dispute. Kiparsky (1991) argues the ordering is essentially unique given economy; Staal and Cardona counter that independent phonetic principles must be doing real work; Petersen (2008) counted millions of equally cheap arrangements. And the puzzle is ancient:

Kātyāyana and Patañjali (the Mahābhāṣya, c. 150 BCE) already asked: why does line 5 read ha ya va ra — could the sounds not stand in another order?

This page answers the checkable core of the question. Take the 29 places where two sounds stand side by side within a line, and swap each pair. Sometimes some class the grammar actually uses stops being contiguous — the order there is forced. Sometimes every class survives (at worst under a new name) — the order there is free, and something other than economy must explain it.

The verdict on the dispute. Economy pins the within-line order at 11 of 29 junctures — including the whole of Patañjali's ha ya va ra — but leaves 18 free. Strikingly, e-before-o and ai-before-au do no grammatical work at all: swap them and every class survives under a new name. So neither side wins outright: the attested class boundaries are rigid (Kiparsky's instinct), while short-before-long and much of the stop order is phonetics or tradition, exactly as Staal and Cardona insisted.

4.What exactly was proved

For each juncture k, either a forced theorem — a witness class plus an exhaustive kernel search over all 42 × 14 = 588 candidate (sound, marker) encodings of the swapped alphabet, proving none names the class — or a free theorem — an explicit surviving pratyāhāra for each of the 43 attested classes, each replayed by decide:

-- forced: swap h‑y and the class yaR (consonants minus h) is unnameable
theorem swap_h_y_breaks : ¬ IsSAlphabetT (swapAdj shivasutras 13) paniniClasses

-- free: swap e‑o and all 43 classes survive (eṄ, eC re-spelled from o)
theorem swap_e_o_free : IsSAlphabetT (swapAdj shivasutras 7) paniniClasses

Formalized in Lean 4 + Mathlib; the result file is panini/Panini/Ordering.lean (the 43 attested classes it quantifies over are defined in Optimality.lean; that file's own optimality theorems live on the result/shivasutra-optimality branch, and the marker-irredundancy theorems on result/marker-irredundancy). All proofs are sorry-free and depend only on the three standard axioms.

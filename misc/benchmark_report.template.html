    Two challenge sets. Two difficulty targets.

    VP-Bench below 70%; VP-Bench-Hard below 50%. Each target applies separately to Opus 5.5 and GPT-6 Astra. Aim for 1,000 unique tasks in each set, with a minimum of 500.

    Calibrate both benchmarks.
    Keep the creation task simple.

    Use development failures to discover grammatical constructions at two difficulty levels. Review the derivations, calibrate the constructions, then sample new cases from reserved families and freeze membership before final evaluation.

    Both sets ask for one accepted Devanagari form from a root and four coordinates: tense/mood, voice, person and number. Same answer-only prompt, no tools, same xHigh / 16,384-token headline protocol.

    VP-Bench / calibrated challenge

    Target: below 70%

    1,000 tasks from challenging constructions. Use the development band above 40% and at most 60% for the stronger reference model within each construction. This leaves margin below the final 70% ceiling.

    Sample diverse qualifying constructions with a fixed seed. Cover at least five tenses/moods and three grammatical phenomena. Prefer one task per root family, with a maximum of two.

    Difficulty comes first, then coverage within the qualifying pool. There is no equal-tense quota or easy-task filler. The legacy 6,687-task report remains a separate reference.
    VP-Bench-Hard / deeper challenge

    Target: below 50%

    1,000 tasks from the hardest qualifying constructions. Require both reference models to score at most 40% in development, leaving margin below the final 50% ceiling.

    Cover at least three tenses/moods and three grammatical phenomena, with the same root-family limits. Use separate task IDs from VP-Bench and preserve valid instances the models ultimately solve.

    The construction definitions and calibration evidence justify the tier. Longer traces, rare roots and model agreement alone do not establish difficulty or correctness.

    The exploration, in six steps

    1. Audit the leads and reserve families.

      Review historical failures, root metadata, variants and gold derivations. Quarantine unresolved cases. Separate final-test families from new calibration and RL training. Each 1,000-task set needs at least 500 families at the two-task cap. Check the available local task space before launching paid discovery.

    2. Search for two levels of difficulty.

      Use up to 6,000 Opus development calls in three batches of 2,000 at High / 4,096 tokens. Allocate 3,000 to failure analogues, 1,500 to unusual rule combinations and 1,500 to coverage controls. Prioritize reviewed leads from the saved runs and locally generated VP traces. After each batch, review valid construction yield and reserved-family supply; stop if both tiers are no longer plausible within the remaining allowances. Controls characterize the search; they do not automatically enter either benchmark. Propose at most ten interpretable construction predicates and resolve overlaps into disjoint cells before fresh calibration.

    3. Calibrate at the final headline settings.

      Build a 1,200-task development panel, initially allocating 600 to each provisional tier. Sample all matching cases, including successes. Cross-check Astra at High / 4,096, then give both models one fresh xHigh / 16,384 attempt. Retained constructions need at least 100 examples, at least 50 root families and ≥95% completed answers per model. Report completion types and uncertainty.

    4. Apply the tier gates and check supply.

      Route constructions using the higher of the two completed-answer accuracies: above 40% through 60% for VP-Bench, at most 40% for Hard. Check the proposed weighted mixtures separately for each model: ≤60% versus ≤40%, with Hard at least 10 percentage points lower in development. Verify enough distinct, reviewed cases and families for both sets before spending the final-evaluation reserve.

    5. Freeze and evaluate both sets.

      Freeze predicates, weights, seed, family exclusions and task IDs. Sample 1,000 new cases per tier, or a documented 500–999 if needed. Run one fresh xHigh / 16,384 attempt per task and model. Report pass@1, root-bootstrap SE, 95% intervals and response failures. The four point-score targets are strict: each model below 70% on VP-Bench and below 50% on Hard.

    6. Check the larger allowance on both tiers.

      Before inspecting final answers, sample 50 VP-Bench and 50 Hard tasks for an additional xHigh / 128,000-token attempt per model. Report separate paired sensitivity results for each tier, including generation noise. Do not replace only failed headline answers. If either target fails or the larger allowance changes difficulty, report it without reselecting final tasks.

    Holdout status must reflect training history. For an existing checkpoint, use families it has not trained on; otherwise train a fresh learner under the new split. Both test sets are disjoint from calibration and learner training, though they may share families with one another. Disclose that overlap. The legacy pool has already been inspected: exclude reserved-family legacy examples from new construction fitting and disclose the earlier exploration.

    Later options
    P0 / VALIDITY BEFORE DIFFICULTY

    Audit the oracle and standardize the prompt.

    Review bhāve feature combinations, root metadata, and accepted variants with a Sanskrit expert. Adjudicate the 68 all-model misses and the 174 answered Opus misses, starting with the bhāve aorist cluster described above. Agreement with Vidyut is the current metric; a disagreement alone does not establish a linguistic mistake.

    Then run every model on the same answer-only prompt, with the same retrieval policy and explicit resource reporting. Preserve refusals, malformed answers and truncations as separate diagnostics.

    Deliverable: versioned task and gold set, written validity rules, common prompt, and a documented correction log. Report legacy and corrected scores separately.
    P1 / DEEPER MORPHOLOGY

    Target the aorist, then add composition.

    After the answer-key audit, use the remaining aorist errors and difficult tense × voice cells as pilot strata. Cover competing stem formations and root exceptions with matched examples. Add prefixes and sanādi operations such as causatives and desideratives, including combinations held out during calibration.

    The current generator samples three bare-root tasks per dictionary entry and has no task fields for these operations. Vidyut supports prefixes and sanādi suffixes, making this extension practical. Generator documentation ↗

    Measure: exact match by operation and interaction; macro-average across predeclared strata. Have experts validate new constructions.
    P1 / CONSISTENCY, NOT ONE LUCKY FORM

    Use contrast sets and paradigm bundles.

    For the same root, vary one valid feature at a time: tense, person, number or voice. Ask for the full contrast set and count the bundle as correct only when every member is correct. This tests whether neighboring forms remain consistent.

    Score both individual forms and complete bundles. As a secondary task, request the full accepted variant set and report precision/recall; this requires a carefully audited, sufficiently complete answer key.

    Measure: form accuracy + all-correct bundle accuracy. The saved three-task bundles already provide a baseline; they are sparse samples, not full paradigms.
    P1 / TRANSFER INTO CONTEXT

    Make the model infer the features.

    Use expert-authored sentence completion and minimal-pair correction tasks where person, number and voice must come from context. Add inverse analysis of a surface form, with context to resolve ambiguity, and score every required feature.

    For derivation tests, ask for a short structured rule trace or a correction to one supplied derivation step. Validate against accepted derivation paths rather than judging free-form explanations.

    Measure: contextual form exact match, feature-set accuracy and verified rule-step accuracy. Keep these as distinct subscores so a harder task cannot hide behind an easy one.
    P0 / A REAL HELD-OUT TEST

    Split by root family before mining failures.

    The original split uses dictionary codes; 11 citation + class groups cross it. Group aliases and related derivations together, then create separate calibration and test partitions before choosing hard items or thresholds.

    Use current model disagreements to design new item families. A challenge subset selected because Opus failed it will have a low Opus score by construction; it cannot independently validate the redesign.

    Measure: frozen unseen-root and unseen-composition scores, paired root-bootstrap intervals, and a separate diagnostic score on previously inspected failures.
    P2 / INFORMATIVE, REPEATABLE SCORING

    Calibrate difficulty across models and budgets.

    08 / METHODS & PROVENANCE

    Reproducible from the saved runs.

    Every displayed score is derived locally. Building this page makes no model requests.

    Scoring and coverage

    The shared parser requires exactly one nonempty <answer> block in visible output. The extracted string is trimmed, NFC-normalized and tested against all saved accepted Devanagari forms. Text outside that block is not penalized. A gold match can count even on a truncated response. Refusals and absent or malformed answers count as failures.

    Current results are one sampled attempt each. Archived pass@1 averages each task’s successes / 16, then averages tasks; it is not pass@16. SFT results cover validation only. These different protocols should not be read as a controlled training ablation.

    Prompt and resource differences

    Anthropic: v2 answer-only prompt, adaptive thinking, 128,000-token output ceiling. OpenAI: v1 requests a derivation, no client output cap. No tools or external retrieval in either run. Equal effort labels are not a common compute budget; per-request latency is not available from these Batch results.

    Uncertainty and scope

    Intervals describe variation over observed root groups conditional on the saved runs. They do not measure repeat-generation variance, provider changes, annotation errors or benchmark selection bias. Subgroup intervals are pointwise, without multiple-comparison adjustment. Small cells and observed extremes need replication. An all-correct or all-wrong slice has a degenerate empirical bootstrap interval; this does not establish certainty on new items.

    Option comparisons are marginal associations. Root mix, other grammatical coordinates, answer-set size and output failures can all contribute. Tense × voice views expose one interaction but do not establish causality.

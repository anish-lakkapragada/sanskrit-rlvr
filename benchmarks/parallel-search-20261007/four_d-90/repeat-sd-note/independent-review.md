# Independent mathematical review

**Pass for the theorem and numerical threshold under the stated assumptions.** This is a methodological result, not an observed repeat SD, a clarification of the user's intended estimand, or a change to the frozen family-precision gate. No final scores were read.

For independent Bernoulli outcomes with fixed per-question probabilities, expanding the sum of variances gives the stated identity and upper bound. Heterogeneous question difficulty is allowed. One-sided Hoeffding bounds the average probability from below using the observed fraction and margin `sqrt(log(1/alpha)/(2N))`. On the coverage event, maximizing `p(1-p)` over `[L,1]` gives exactly the note's piecewise upper variance bound. The one-sided, independent-but-not-identically-distributed form is explicit in [CMU section 5.2](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~larry/=stat700/Lecture5.pdf) and [Isabelle's independent bounded-variable theorem](https://isabelle.in.tum.de/library/HOL/HOL-Probability/Hoeffding.html).

With N=1800 and alpha=.025, the margin is **.032010759710664**. SD below .01 requires `L > .764575131106459`, hence a known-correct count strictly greater than **1433.8546034708215**. The first sufficient integer is **1434**: its bound is **0.9998812455 percentage points**, versus **1.0006969915** for 1433. The two-model union bound gives at least 95% simultaneous repeated-experiment coverage without requiring independence between models.

The missingness argument is also valid for its stated latent target: if known positives K are pointwise no greater than the full latent success sum T, then `{L(K)>pbar}` is contained in `{L(T)>pbar}`. Missingness need not be independent of outcomes for this inequality. However, it requires a well-defined independent latent response vector and genuinely verified positives. It does not establish either assumption or bound the operational variance created by correlated outages.

I added the two parent-authorized clarifications to the README before fingerprinting it: coverage is **not 95% conditional on passing the threshold**, and the latent complete-answer SD differs from delivered-score SD under infrastructure failures. As an illustrative counterexample to ignoring dependence, a common run-level success probability of .78 or .82, each with probability one half, gives marginal accuracy .8 but fixed-panel repeat SD about **2.21 percentage points** at N=1800. A single panel cannot rule out such shared variation.

Independent discrete convolution checks at N=1800 confirmed the coverage direction for homogeneous .8 probabilities and a .75/.85 mixture, with and without one hidden item.

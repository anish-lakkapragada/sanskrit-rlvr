# Bounded primary-source review of the 96-family ending screen

The four grammatical predicates have primary-commentary support. All 96 selected inputs regenerate under current policy21 and local holds; all 124 accepted VP derivation paths satisfy the actual local conditions below. This establishes the intended rule interaction and preserves the complete **engine** union. It does not independently certify every dictionary meaning, stem operation or classical spelling.

The independent source review found a real accepted-spelling gap: optional consonant doubling is not present in the engine keys. Canonical gold is preserved. A separate, task-specific alias whitelist will cover independently supported alternatives before evaluation; the primary screen must use that whitelist and report canonical scoring alongside it. No general consonant-collapse normalization is proposed.

## Accepted-spelling issue and limits

Kāśikā [8.4.47](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.47.htm) explicitly extends optional yar doubling to word end, with tat/tatt and ṣaṭ/ṣaṭṭ. Bālamanoramā [8.4.56](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.56.htm) describes singleton/doubled t and d, rather than only the engine's t/d voicing alternatives. The same final yar condition includes dental n. These pages sometimes have a heading number one lower than the Kāśikā section; URLs and rule names resolve the intended passage.

For example, canonical alUzIt/alUzId have supported alUzItt/alUzIdd aliases. All 12 selected third-singular aorists have the final-stop issue. The 24 secondary-plural questions have final n. Canonical engine forms are never overwritten, and aliases never enter the model prompt.

Kāśikā/Bālamanoramā [8.4.46](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.46.htm) also support doubling a following consonant after postvocalic r/h, e.g. arka/arkka and brahma/brahmma. Internal examples in this panel include anardIt/anarddIt and arcitAsi/arccitAsi. The scope must preserve the prohibition on doubling r/h themselves, the śar-before-vowel restriction in [8.4.49](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.49.htm), and the nuances of existing clusters in [8.4.50](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.50.htm). [8.4.51](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/8/8.4.51.htm) supports the undoubled branch. General 8.4.47 has additional internal applications; the whitelist documents its exact finite scope and unresolved contexts rather than claiming an exhaustive spelling engine.

Known licensed aliases must be fixed before outputs. Any unforeseen candidate spelling requires separately reported, source-based adjudication applied symmetrically, with the original canonical and preregistered alias-aware results retained. An unseen spelling must not automatically become evidence of morphological difficulty.

## Difficulty evidence

All 19 earlier usable Opus development examples meeting the strict four-cell predicate were correct. They are sparse, selected historical coverage across mixed token protocols; no numerical below-90% forecast is justified. The new screen has six tenses, two persons, singular/plural and Kartari only, despite all ten classes and 96 distinct families. Passing a small development screen would motivate a separately designed confirmation and reference comparison, not qualify a final benchmark or establish sub-1pp precision.

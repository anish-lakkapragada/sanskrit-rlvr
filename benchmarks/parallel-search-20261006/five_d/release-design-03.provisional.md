The 2,400-question allocation necessarily has 441 repeated aorists across 441 families; larger allocations require three or four aorists in some families. These are availability witnesses, not selected panels or guarantees of independent questions.

Both reference models need a precision assessment. At p=.65 and N=2,400, a common family correlation of only .05 already gives SE above 1pp. At N=3,840, rho=.2 still gives about1.025pp. The larger N options therefore reduce risk but cannot guarantee the requested precision; the reported SE must come from the actual final family allocation. The two-question, different-tense Opus pilot estimate cannot simply be reused for repeated-aorist families.

The strongest reference point accuracy must lie in79–81%; all-request and completed scores and missing-response bounds must agree on that conclusion. A95% interval is reported, but it is not required to fit inside the2pp target band. This distinguishes a point-score target with an SE threshold from the much stronger claim of tightly certifying the population accuracy.

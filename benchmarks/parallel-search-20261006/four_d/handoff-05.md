## Confirmed lead and its limits

Pilot05 completed all 52 fresh questions: 43 correct, nine source-supported disagreements in five families, no refusal or truncation. Its predeclared gate of at most 44 correct and at least five error families passes. The non-aorist contrast is 31/39 (79.49%), while aorist is 12/13 (92.31%). The whole panel therefore remains above target at 82.69%.

The lead is defined phonologically within dictionary adanta Curadi, not by a list of failed roots. All 13 eligible development families were included equally; 12 appeared in pilot04. Estimated family-cluster SE for the non-aorist result is 8.04 percentage points. This confirms transfer to fresh tuples within mostly familiar development families, not transfer to independent lexical families. The test predicate has 1,134 non-aorist tuples but only 14 families; the distinct RL predicate has 729 tuples across nine families. No test answers were read.

All nine errors are explained by primary lexical/stem controls and the rule treatment of original vowels after deletion. The audit proposes no policy change. Representative controls and explicit inference are not full independent certification of every accepted variant. ## Best mixture calculations

The updated [joint frontier](pilot-04.mixture-frontier-r3-policy13.json) uses only test metadata capacities and development grammar-cell rates. It allows fractional task counts and saves no final membership. Its universe is a tested-predicate union, not all Sanskrit.

At N=1,000, the lowest fixed-rate calculation is 78.91%, entirely Curadi. Representing every class with one question still leaves 991 Curadi questions and gives 79.08%. Requiring, illustratively, five classes each to contribute 5% gives 82.86%, still with 79.5% Curadi. A six-per-family sensitivity gives 94.33%. At N=1,660, even the unrestricted fixed-rate calculation is 82.06%. These examples jointly impose their stated constraints; no equal quotas or numerical class/family cap are attributed to the user.

The policy13 recomputation preserves all numerical mixture rates and all 474 test families. It removes 89 background test tuples (15,818 to 15,729 total), because excluding the development svid citation also removes its test counterpart's eligibility for the marker-contrast predicate. That counterpart remains grammatical and retains independently eligible questions. Pilot03's ordinary score is now 174/175; its unresolved-stim sensitivity baseline is 174/174 instead of 175/175, so the optimizer's baseline rate remains 1.0. Pilot02 is now 103/103 completed and 103/104 overall. Pilots04/05 are unchanged. The [current shared-summary snapshot](current-evidence-policy13.json) records the source hash.

The calculation is deliberately favorable to finding hardness: it retains the post-hoc, unconfirmed 10/13 rate for other-adanta aorists and assumes development rates transfer to test families. It is not a forecast, a statistical lower bound, or proof that another grammar mechanism cannot work. Rates based on 13–39 questions have substantial uncertainty.

For scale, even assigning half a benchmark to the confirmed non-aorist rate of 79.49% would require the other half to average about 80.51% to reach 80%. The broad tested components instead scored approximately 97–100%. A second independently supported hard mechanism with substantial distinct family/class supply is needed; adding easy diversity cannot be assumed harmless.

## Dialect alternative

The [offline Vedic inspection](search-05.vedic-alternative.json) records installed Vidyut 0.4.0 signatures and an immutable source snapshot. A global `is_chandasi=True` option exists. The inspected source distinguishes present-, aorist- and perfect-stem Let; the installed Python Tinanta signature does not expose the stem selector. Some source branches explicitly discuss corpus-based alternatives whose Paninian licensing or subjunctive/imperative analysis is uncertain.

A clearly declared global dialect and stem policy could in principle preserve exactly four per-question coordinates. It would change the present contract and still require source-certified variant coverage, lexical applicability and fresh calibration. Merely toggling the flag does not establish these. No Let questions, hidden dialect choices, policy change or paid Vedic request are proposed.

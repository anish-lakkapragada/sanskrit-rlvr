# Ordinary 4D coverage diagnostic

**Verdict: one bounded 96-question exploration is justified.** Athematic first-person dual/plural imperatives combine the p-it prescription of 3.4.92 with three distinct strong/weak stem systems. The relevant interaction has substantial development-family breadth and almost no coverage in either the five current xHigh pilots or the broader legacy development inputs. This establishes an unanswered grammar-coverage question, not evidence that the model will fail or that an 80% benchmark is feasible.

The exact classical task contract, shared policy21, original 4D renderer and development/test/RL family split remain unchanged. No model-answer files were opened for this phase; no API calls or shared edits were made. Reserved-test access was limited to aggregate input supply, explicitly requested by the coordinator. No test membership was selected.

## The source-defined interaction

[Kāśikā and Nyāsa 3.4.92](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/3/3.4.92.htm) assign p-it to the first-person imperative ending. Nyāsa explicitly connects that assignment to preventing ṅ-it treatment and permitting guṇa. The condition therefore changes morphology beyond adding an imperative ending.

The [source audit](pilot-06.source-audit.json) distinguishes three branches: guṇa permitted in the strong stem; nasal-infix a retained because [6.4.111](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.4.111.htm) requires k/ṅ-it; and śnā/reduplicated ā retained because [6.4.112](https://sanskritdocuments.org/~sanskrit/learning_tools/sarvanisutrani/6.4.112.htm) and [6.4.113](https://sanskritdocuments.org/learning_tools/ashtadhyayi/vyakhya/6/6.4.113.htm) require k/ṅ-it for the weak changes. These are source-rule inferences supported by direct contrasting paradigms, not separately printed attestations of every selected form. Eighteen saved [positive, weak and negative controls](pilot-06.source-controls.json) preserve complete derivation paths.

Stumbh/skumbh retain both śnā and śnu alternatives.

The [analysis declaration](analysis-predeclaration.json) and tested analyzer require the full named 96-question job to be settled and collected before reading results. They report target and control all-request/usable scores separately, whole-family paired intervals, both-completed comparisons and completion bounds. The exploratory trigger requires at least 44 usable answers per role, wrong completed targets in at least eight families, three classes and two branches, and at least four more wrong targets than controls. Missing controls receive a separate worst-case sensitivity. A trigger can justify later calibration only after source review; it cannot select individual failures or authorize release.

**Scaling remains restrictive.** Only 156 test target tuples across 78 families are available; this stratum cannot supply 500 questions alone. At 80%, even an independent 48-target diagnostic has about 5.8 pp nominal standard error. There is no final accuracy, reference-order or sub-one-point precision claim, and no benchmark is ready.
